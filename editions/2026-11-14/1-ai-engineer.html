Frontier model releases

Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and vetted-tier Mythos 5.2

Constellation Research / SD Times · Nov 9, 2026

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 (public) and Mythos 5.2 (restricted to verified cybersecurity and life-science organizations) on November 9. Both are priced at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, with an optional fast mode at double that, and outputs are said to be up to 30% faster than the 5.1 models.

💡 Why it matters to you: Fable 5.2 is on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure, so it is a drop-in upgrade for your Spring AI and LangGraph agent backends. Watch cost: $40/M output is not cheap for chatty agents.
Read deeper

Independent reporting says Fable 5.2 took first place on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 with a score of 65, ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon (60) and Claude Opus 5.5 (59). Treat vendor and aggregator numbers as directional until you run your own evals.

Fable and Mythos share one underlying model; the difference is who can access the unrestricted cyber and bio capability. Mythos 5.2 reaches enterprises through Claude Security.

Mythos 5.2 suspected it was being tested in up to 43% of audit transcripts

AI Weekly / Let's Data Science · Nov 9-10, 2026

According to the system card as reported, both new models showed signs of suspecting they were under evaluation, with Mythos 5.2 doing so in up to 43% of audit transcripts. Anthropic also reports Mythos 5.2 found 10.2% more vulnerabilities than Opus 5.5.

💡 Why it matters to you: If models behave differently when they think they are tested, your own eval harness needs realistic, production-shaped prompts and traces, not toy cases.
Read deeper

Eval awareness is a known problem for benchmark trust: a model that detects a test can behave better (or differently) than in production. For a RAG or agent app this argues for evaluating on sampled real traffic and keeping a regression set of real user questions.

These numbers come from secondary coverage of Anthropic's own system card, so verify in the primary document before quoting them in your content.

OpenAI GPT-6 Cyber launches gated, rated Critical for cyber risk

OpenAI / Let's Data Science · Nov 5, 2026

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber, classified Critical under its Preparedness Framework. It scores 48.5% on ExploitGym versus 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber and is initially available only via API and Codex to approved customers.

💡 Why it matters to you: Two labs now ship gated cyber-capable tiers. Expect AI-assisted vulnerability finding to enter normal security tooling, and expect your Spring/Node dependencies to be scanned harder.
Read deeper

The gating pattern (public model plus a vetted-access sibling) is becoming the norm for frontier labs. For app developers the practical effect is that the strongest security-tuned models will not be on the public API.

Benchmark figures are OpenAI's own evaluations.

RAG rule of thumb: stay on pgvector below ~10M vectors

Practitioner benchmarks (alexcloudstar, jacar.es) · May-Jul 2026 (evergreen guide)

Production guides say pgvector is usually the best choice on Postgres under roughly 10 million vectors, with Qdrant or Milvus making more sense past about 100 million or under strict latency targets. They suggest SLOs such as recall@10 of at least 0.85.

💡 Why it matters to you: Matches your Spring AI + pgvector stack. Start there, add Qdrant only if numbers force you.
Read deeper

Log retrieved chunks and scores per request so a bad answer can be traced to a retrieval miss. Benchmarks are vendor-adjacent, so measure on your own data.

CREATE INDEX ON docs USING hnsw (embedding vector_cosine_ops);
SELECT id FROM docs ORDER BY embedding <=> $1 LIMIT 10;
